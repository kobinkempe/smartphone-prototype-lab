# Magnet making facts for this review

Current UI rules are in REVISION-2.md: Parts and Tools only; all tool-use instructions belong in the caller, with manufacturing on the tool's own page.

Arnold Magnetic Technologies describes sintered magnet production as powder preparation, alignment/compaction, sintering/heat treatment, finishing and magnetization. Therefore a finished permanent magnet is not merely a shaped piece of iron, and a press, furnace, finishing equipment and magnetizer are separate manufacturing tools. Different compositions and processes need different equipment and requirements; these navigation samples do not supply a manufacturing specification.

https://www.arnoldmagnetics.com/resources/magnet-manufacturing-process/

TDK distinguishes hard ferrite permanent magnets from soft ferrite used for cores. Hard ferrite is formed and sintered, then magnetized using a strong external field. Do not call soft ferrite a permanent magnet, or imply an iron flux return is the permanent-field source. The iron pole/return and permanent magnet are distinct parts with distinct making chains.

https://www.tdk.com/en/tech-mag/ferrite02/001
